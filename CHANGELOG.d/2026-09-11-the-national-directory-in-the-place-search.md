---
date: 2026-09-11
title: "\"Not ours, but here\" — the national directory of local bus maps now answers a search that ours cannot"
---

Until today a reader searching `/maps` for somewhere we have no map for got the sentence *No matches* and nothing to click. The portal now answers the question that miss leaves behind — *then does anybody publish one?* — from the directory of every English local transport authority built in buses-data [OA-308](https://github.com/PeterC66/community-bus-maps) tier 1 and finished on 11 September 2026: 76 authorities, 48 of which publish a map of their whole network and 49 of which publish nothing at town level.

- **A second panel below the grid, never mixed in with it.** Directory rows are somebody else's maps and the markup says so three times over: a *Not ours* badge, a quieter card with no screenshot, and the line **"Published by &lt;authority&gt;, last checked on &lt;date&gt;"** in full. We vouch for one thing — that the map was there on the date shown — and the card says that in as many words. Links carry `rel="nofollow noopener"`.
- **Matched on the authority and on area names, and on nothing else.** There is no place-to-authority dataset here, so a query we cannot place is an honest miss: the panel says we looked, says we cannot tell which authority covers that place, and suggests searching the county instead. It never guesses a plausible county. Substring matching is deliberately off — *ton* matching eleven authorities is noise.
- **Two fields were added to the directory data because the first build of this panel was misleading without them.** `covers[]` carries the constituent councils of a combined authority and the districts each 2023 unitary replaced, so *Derbyshire*, *Wigan* and *Allerdale* reach the authority that now runs their buses instead of matching nothing at all. `alsoPublished[]` carries a map published by a DIFFERENT public authority for part of an area: without it, a reader searching **York** — the enquiry that started the whole directory — was told the combined authority publishes no bus map, which is true of the authority and wrong about the city. The card now reads *"But: City of York Council publishes one for York"*, with the link.
- **An authority that publishes nothing still gets a card.** It is the most useful row on the page for somebody about to ask their council for a map, and filtering it out would leave the reader thinking we had not looked.
- **Server-rendered as well as client-rendered**, from the same shared markup module, for the reason the grid already was: the one page where a reader most needs a second answer must not be the page a crawler and a JS-off reader see "Loading" on.
- **Vendored as a projection, not a copy.** `public/data/bus-map-directory.json` holds the fields the panel renders and deliberately not the survey's notes or its sources — those are our own candid assessment of named councils' websites, written in a private repository, and this one is public. `npm run sync:directory` writes it; `-- --check` compares it byte for byte with the projection of the source and runs in `verify.yml`, the one workflow with a buses-data checkout. `npm test` asserts the shape instead and says in its own header that it cannot ask the staleness question.
- **Tested and falsified.** `scripts/test-directory.mjs` covers the file's shape, the matching rules, the three promises the card makes and the York and Southend cases; `scripts/prove-red-directory.mjs` breaks seven things on purpose in a scratch copy and requires each named check to go red. Arm 6 found a real hole on its first run: the "this date comes from the data, not the clock" check passed even with the clock patched in, because every row was surveyed on the day it was written — the two values were the same string. It uses a deliberately back-dated row now.
