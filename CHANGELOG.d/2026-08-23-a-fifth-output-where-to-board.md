---
date: 2026-08-23
title: "A fifth output: 'Where to board'"
---

- **The portal now offers the boarding plan.** Step 3 of `Development Docs/boarding-plan-product_2026-08-22.md`, and the last of its three. The sheet itself was built and prototyped on four places between 22 and 23 August; what was missing was everything on this side of the fence. It is one entry in `OUTPUTS` (`src/maps/store.js`), as that paper predicted — `base: 'boarding'`, `label: 'Where to board'`, `engine: 'expert'`, `requiresConfig: 'boardingPlan'`, `requestOnly: true` — and the whole downstream chain (render, version, publish, downloads, the customer's toggles, the public page) follows from the registry with no further change.
- **What the sheet is.** The other four answer *where do the buses go*. This one answers the inverse, which is the question a passenger standing in a bus station actually has: *I have decided to go to Bedford — which of these five identically-named stops do I stand at?* A large-scale locator of one place, and an index of destinations **sorted by destination, not by route number**, each row naming the boarding point. Every stand letter is printed verbatim from NaPTAN, so the letter on the sheet is the letter on the flag.
- **`requestOnly`, for a different reason from the diagram's.** Not hand-placed pins — the frame radius, the empty-stand rule and the locator's landmarks are per-place judgements, and the sheet carries the refresh obligation harder than anything else we make: a plan on a pole that disagrees with the flag beside it is worse than no plan. A customer sees it locked with *Ask us*; an admin grants it. `chooseOutputs()` already enforced that, and `test-p7.mjs` now asserts it for this output too.
- **`requiresFiles` — a new gate, one level below `requiresConfig`.** The generator reads `stands.json` and `boarding_index.json`, which only a place built through the skill's Phase 3 carries, and it **exits non-zero** when they are missing — which would have failed the whole map's render rather than this one sheet. `resolveGen()` now reports an output whose required data files are absent as *unavailable*, exactly as an absent config key already did. Checked for every output, not only the expert ones.
- **The generator had never been runnable outside the skill's own folder.** `gen_boarding.js` required `footer.js`, `icons.js` and `font_metrics.js` from `__dirname` alone — true only while it sits beside them in `make-bus-leaflet/assets`. The portal runs a generator from `engine/expert/` against a map's data folder and passes `SKILL_ASSETS`, so this would have thrown on the *require*, before reading a single input. It now self-resolves the same way `gen_internal.js` has for months (sibling → `SKILL_ASSETS` → the skill path), and honours `LEAFLET_DIR` rather than resting on the portal happening to spawn with `cwd` set to the data dir.
- **A customer's recolour now reaches this sheet.** `gen_boarding.js` read its palette from `routes.json` and nothing else, so a route recoloured in the editor would have printed in the new colour on three sheets and the old one on the fourth — in a single leaflet. It now reads the same `routeColors` key from the same overrides file `gen_internal.js` reads. **`hiddenOperators` is deliberately NOT applied**: which stand a destination is boarded at was decided by `boarding_index.py` across every route serving it, so dropping routes in the generator can strand a destination that is still reachable from another stand. The generator refuses (under `STRICT_GUARDS`) instead of half-applying it.
- **`STRICT_GUARDS` now covers this generator too.** Its five "I could not draw what you asked for" paths — a marker pushed further than its own radius, a stand key line wider than the column, stands that do not fit under the map, an index note that runs into the print-safe margin, destinations that did not fit — wrote to stderr and exited 0, and `renderMap.js` reads stderr only on a non-zero exit. They are counted and become the exit code. **Silent on all four existing sheets**, so the gate starts green; proved red by giving a fixture a 400-character note.
- **Proved, not asserted.** `verify:place` now runs **every** committed place fixture, not just the first — it took `fixtures[0]` from the day the list existed, which was harmless with one fixture and would have quietly skipped the second. `buses-data` commits a new one, `Places/_portal-fixture/High Wycombe High Street`, whose 54-destination boarding sheet the portal reproduces **byte-identically (67,252 B) and pixel-identically**. Watched go red on a one-character change to the vendored engine, and green again on restoring it. The four sheets built by the skill were re-rendered before and after the generator changes and came out byte-for-byte unchanged.
- **The shopfront says so.** The home page's *Four outputs* section is *Five*, with the boarding plan given a wide card of its own beneath the four — it answers a different question from its siblings and does not belong in their row. New FAQ entry (`/faq.html#boarding`), a worked example on `/examples.html#boarding` (High Wycombe High Street, six stands, 54 destinations), and a *Costs Extra* line on `/pricing.html` that says what it is and where it cannot be offered. **No price is quoted** — that decision is open, and the pricing page is honest about the pilot.
- **Where it cannot be offered, and the page says it.** Stand lettering is an authority's policy, not a national standard: Buckinghamshire letters its town-centre streets in one town-wide series, Cambridgeshire letters only its bus stations, and some places letter nothing. Where the stops around a place cannot each be named unambiguously the generator declines rather than guess, and `requiresConfig` turns that into an output the customer is simply not offered.
- **LIVE the same day.** <https://busmaps.uk/m/st-neots-town-centre> **v6.1** carries the first published boarding sheet. The sequence is worth recording because it is not one act: accept the delivered update (which is what puts `boardingPlan` into the map's LIVE `routes.json`), *then* grant `boarding_plan` — it cannot be granted before, the toggle is gated on the live data — *then* save a version, because `PATCH outputs` sets a flag and renders nothing. Only then submit and approve.
- **Still to come, and not blocked by this.** `/examples.html` shows a static High Wycombe example plus a link to the live St Neots one — High Wycombe is the harder case and the better example, so that is deliberate rather than a stopgap. The two live boarding sheets (St Ives Bus Station, St Neots Town Centre) are a re-render behind the engine, which is a re-delivery decision. And `/m/<slug>/services` — the accessible text equivalent — does not yet carry the boarding index, which is the one place this sheet would beat a PDF spider map outright.
