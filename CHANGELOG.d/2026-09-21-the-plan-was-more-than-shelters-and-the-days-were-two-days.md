- **Three claims on the public site catch up with the DfT's Bus NAP guidance, published 28 August 2026** (buses-data OA-094, `Bus policy position 2026-08-17.md` §5). `public/faq.html#councils` said a bus network accessibility plan "has to assess bus stop infrastructure: shelters, kerbs, lighting" and that we do not hold that — true of one component out of the guidance's recommended nine, three of which (information and journey planning; network design and service availability; signage and at-stop information) are exactly what this project holds. The answer now says so, and still says plainly that the infrastructure audit itself is not ours.
- **`public/opportunity.html`'s "Why now" paragraph asserted one deadline where the guidance gives two.** The Enhanced Partnership plan update is due 31 March 2027; the first bus network accessibility plan is due 1 April 2027 — a day later, not the same day. Both dates are now given separately.
- **Peter's decision: quote the DfT on print, on this page only.** The guidance asks every English LTA to offer its accessibility guide "as an accessible website and a hard-copy leaflet available on vehicles, travel offices, at transport interchanges and council offices, to reach as many disabled passengers as possible" — a *should*, not a *must*, and about the accessibility guide, not a bus map. The "Why now" paragraph now quotes it verbatim, with the link and the publication date, and makes this project's own claim explicitly: a printed map drawn from the same stop and service data belongs beside that guide. `public/faq.html#print` and the councils card on `public/pricing.html` are unchanged on Peter's instruction — the print argument there stays "ours rather than the government's".
