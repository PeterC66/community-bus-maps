---
date: 2026-08-28
title: "Backup retention is days again, not folders — the VPS window had quietly shrunk to 25 hours"
---

- **`--keep 14` meant fourteen FOLDERS, and four documents described it as fourteen days.** The old rule sorted the backups directory, sliced at 14 and deleted the tail. That is a fortnight only if the nightly cron is the sole thing writing snapshots, and it is not: `npm run deploy` takes one before every release and anybody can take one by hand before a risky change. On 2026-08-27 that happened a dozen times in a working day, and by the next morning the host held `2026-08-27T12-48-05` through `2026-08-28T13-29-23` — **the entire retention window was 25 hours**, measured on the live host, with the fortnight of nightlies gone.
- **Retention is now by age, in [`src/ops/backup-retention.js`](src/ops/backup-retention.js), with two guards that keep it bounded.** Everything newer than `--keep-recent-hours` (48) is kept whole, so the backup taken right before a deploy is still there while that deploy is being judged; beyond that only the newest snapshot of each UTC day survives, so a burst of manual runs costs one day of history rather than a fortnight of it; and the newest `--keep` (7) are held whatever their age, so an age rule cannot empty the directory at the moment those copies are the only ones left. `--keep` is now a floor, not the window. `compose.yaml` passes `--keep-days 14 --keep 7`.
- **The cost was real and is not recoverable.** A laptop-side pull on 2026-08-27 was killed mid-`scp` and left `2026-08-27T03-15-02` holding a manifest, one map folder of twenty and **no database at all** — 4.1 MB where a snapshot is 316 MB. By the time it was noticed the host's own retention had already rolled past that timestamp, so the whole snapshot could not be re-fetched from anywhere. The newest complete off-box copy of the database was two days older than every check believed. Either fault alone would have been survivable; it took both.
- **[`scripts/test-backup-retention.mjs`](scripts/test-backup-retention.mjs) is in the `npm test` chain, and its last case is a control rather than a test.** It re-implements the old count-only rule over the same fabricated names and asserts the two rules **disagree**. Without it every other case here would still pass if someone restored the old behaviour, because on most days the two agree — 2026-08-27 was not most days. Proven both ways: the module was reverted to `slice(14)` on purpose and seven assertions went red including the control, then restored and all sixteen went green.
- **`docs/DEPLOY.md`'s erasure procedure was computing a date off the wrong number.** Step 4 of the right-to-erasure runbook says a removal completes when the last backup holding the person rotates out, "14 days on the VPS" — which on the morning this was found meant 25 hours. Corrected there, and in `README.md`, `docs/H1-operations-handbook.md` and `docs/ROADMAP.md`, all of which quoted `--keep 14` as a duration.
