title: "The unsent-draft row told the operator what the public has after consulting one of the four clauses that decide it — and it was most reassuring exactly where a map had been taken down"
---

- **`"Ramsey" has an unsent draft (v8.0) — the public still has v7.0`, while `/m/ramsey` was a 404.** `listUnsubmittedDrafts()` selected `pv.storage_key AS published_key` off a `LEFT JOIN` on `published_version_id` and filtered on `m.status <> 'archived'`, and `src/worklist/index.js` rendered *the public still has …* from that one column. `PUBLIC_WHERE` — twenty lines away in the same file — wants four clauses: a published version, `public_listed = 1`, an active customer, and a non-archived map. *Published* and *public* read as synonyms in English and are not synonyms in the schema, and nothing in the row's own file said so. Recorded as buses-data OA-295, found on 2026-09-10 while working the Ramsey re-listing.

- **The sentence is worst where it is most reassuring.** A map goes un-listed for one reason — something on it was wrong enough to take down — so `public_listed = 0` marks an open incident, and this row told the reader that incident's map was serving the public a version it was not serving. For a day it said so about the one map in the estate the public could not load at all.

- **The query now selects `m.public_listed` and the customer's status, and the row says one of three things**: *the public still has v7.0*, *this map has never been published*, or **NOT LISTED — the public sees nothing**, which is a different and more urgent claim than either. The *why* names which clause is holding it down — an un-listing or a suspension — because nothing on the map itself records the second.

- **It also leaves the tidy-up band.** An unsent draft on a listed map is housekeeping and ages from rank 9 to 8 after a week, correctly: a draft blocks nobody. On an **un-listed** map the draft is the thing that would end an incident, so it is rank 4 immediately, in *Your move* rather than *Waiting on others*. Its do-step says re-listing is a second act — publishing a version does not undo an un-listing, and the page stays a 404 until somebody does.

- **Proved able to fail, and the fourth mutation is the one that matters.** `scripts/prove-red-unlisted-draft.mjs` restores the bug verbatim, the half-fix that consults `public_listed` and forgets the customer, and the wording-fixed-but-rank-unmoved case; each reddens the assertion that names it, with the unmutated copy green. The fourth drops the two new columns from the SQL, so `undefined` reads falsy and **every** published map shouts NOT LISTED — and three of the four wording cases still pass under it. That is the control direction: these are assertions about the wording of a sentence, and a row that shouted at every draft would have satisfied most of them.

- **Not done, and left as a paragraph on OA-295 rather than a second change.** `PUBLIC_COLUMNS` aliases `pv.created_at AS published_at`, so `/api/public/maps` reports `publishedAt` as the version row's creation time — Ramsey's says 2026-09-08, and it was published on the 10th. No public page renders it, so nothing a reader sees is wrong; but the choice between `pr.reviewed_at` and renaming the field to `versionCreatedAt` changes a published API contract either in its value or in its name, and that is not a change to make silently alongside a worklist fix.
