date: 2026-09-13
title: "The licensing record did not know the licensor had answered, and the answer had been in the other repository for a day"
---

**`docs/LICENSING.md` now records what the OpenStreetMap Foundation actually said, and it is the only row in that document that did not come from us.** The operator put four questions to the OSMF **Licensing Working Group** on 25 August 2026 — whether map-matching bus routes onto the OSM road network creates a Derivative Database, whether the bounded extract behind a boarding plan's locator map sits differently, whether **ODbL §4.6** obliges us to offer derived data on the strength of publishing a Produced Work made from it, and whether supplying geometry to a customer's own designer changes the position. They answered on 12 September: map-matching is a **Trivial Transformation**, the locator extract *"does sound large enough to be considered Substantial"* but *"since you are only Publicly distributing the Produced Works … the only obligation is attribution"*, **§4.6 is *"moot if you're not making alterations"***, and *"whether money changes hands does not matter"*. It is a new subsection under §1, the same kind of record as the bustimes.org resolution at §3, with a dated row in the §5 review table beside it.

**Nothing in this file was wrong, which is exactly what made it easy to leave.** The OSM row already said the maps are Produced Works, that the credit is the main obligation, and that a published derived database would have to be offered under the ODbL — and the Working Group confirmed all three. What was missing was that anybody had asked and that an answer existed, so a reader of the file that is supposed to be the licensing record — a customer's solicitor, a procurement officer, or a session answering a licensing question in a hurry — would have concluded the §4.6 question was still open. **A record whose gap is an absence rather than an error is the hardest kind to notice**, because every sentence in it reads true.

**Three practical statements come out of it, and the second changes how one line of code should be thought about.** Nothing is owed beyond the attribution already printed, so the §4.6 question that was open at the last review is closed. **`OUTPUT_FILES` is a licence control and not only a tidiness one**: it is an allowlist, `src/server.js` gates both file routes on it, and it is what keeps `locator_geo.json` and `routes_paths.json` inside every delivered payload and off the wire — so widening it to serve a geometry file is a licensing decision rather than a feature, because those files would then have to carry the ODbL, which the Working Group said in as many words. And **the first answer rests on a fact about the generator rather than about its output**: we select OSM geometry and simplify it, never edit it, and if that ever changed the *Trivial Transformation* answer would stop describing what we do with nothing here going red.

**One sentence of theirs is flagged as not to be repeated to a customer, and that flag is the point of writing this down rather than filing it.** Answering the fourth question they said a customer designer's own map *"would need to be ODbL licenced"*. That reaches further than **ODbL §4.5**, which exempts a Produced Work from share-alike, and further than their own answer to the second question, which puts the licence on the extract *files*. A map drawn from OSM data owes attribution, not share-alike — the difference between *use this data and credit OpenStreetMap* and *publish your design under an open licence*, only the first of which is correct or sellable. That single point has gone back to the Working Group and is not yet answered.

**`/legal.html` was checked and deliberately not changed, and that is recorded in the file.** It describes the sheets we publish, says the geography is OpenStreetMap under the ODbL, and asks readers not to crop the credits; every claim on it survives the answer unaltered. *"We looked and nothing needed changing"* is otherwise indistinguishable from not having looked, which is the same failure this entry is about one level down.

**Where the evidence lives, and why it is not here.** Both letters, the reply verbatim and a row-by-row triage of where each answer went are in `Correspondence/CORR-006/` in the private `buses-data` repository; this repository carries the summary its own reader needs and short quotations of the operative phrases, attributed to the Working Group rather than to the individual who signed. The answer is an informal view, is expressly not legal advice, and does not bind the Foundation — the caveat this document carries everywhere else.
