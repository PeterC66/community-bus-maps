date: 2026-08-30
title: "A number where the name would not go"
---

- **Every label the placer drops is now offered an ordinal instead, and the names are listed under the Key as *Numbered on the map*.** 288 labels across the 52 committed sheets could not be placed, and every one of them left its ICON on the map: an anonymous supermarket trolley, an anonymous mortarboard, a park with no name. The reader could not tell what it was and could not tell that anything was missing either. `labeller.js`'s new `indexPass()` re-runs the same solver over the failures with the text replaced by a number — *"Cambridge Fish Preservation and Angling Society Ltd. Managed Fishery"* is 84 mm of type and `17` is 2.6 — so a place with nowhere for its name usually has somewhere for its number.
- **Board-wide it identifies 85 of the 263 places the internal-family sheets were drawing anonymously, and the split is the finding.** Outside High Wycombe's three town sheets it is 68 of 98; on those three it is 17 of 165, because that sheet has no room beside its symbols even for two digits. The count it could not number goes to `stderr` and into `build-warnings.txt` rather than being silently halved, which is a sheet saying out loud that it is the wrong size — and the first hard evidence for the multi-sheet High Wycombe that has been argued for since the town-complexity triage.
- **It does not force, and that is the load-bearing decision.** A point with no room keeps its silence, because a number stamped on a route line is one a reader can neither read nor look up — forcing would have driven the drop count to zero and bought a ratcheted measure with a definition change. For the same reason `unplaced.json` now holds the RESIDUE and a new `indexed.json` holds what was numbered, and the build line states both.
- **The index is numbered alphabetically and laid out in the fewest columns that hold it.** A reader arrives at the list from two directions — with a number, wanting a name, and with a name, wanting to know whether the map shows it — and an alphabetical list ordered by its own numbers answers both. Every column past the first takes width from every entry, so a five-row list keeps the full panel width: the first cut chose the columns before it knew the row count and shortened *St Mary and All Saints CofE Primary* to fit a half-width column it did not need.
- **The compass gets a second look once the labels are down.** `north_arrow.js` sites itself before the solve, which is right — the arrow can go anywhere and a label cannot. What it cannot see from there is a `mustPlace` destination caption, costed heavily for entering a reserved box and never dropped for it, so the arrow took the blank corner and *to Chatteris* was printed through the N on Ely Co-op internal anyway. It now re-sites when a label has actually landed on it, and only if somewhere clear is left. A hand-pinned arrow is never moved.
- **`mapNotes` gains a wrap width.** Ely Co-op's AJ2 footnote is authored at `x: 6`, which is the *largest* available width there is, so no smaller `x` could make it wrap and its 120 characters ran 130 mm across the sheet and through a frame-exit badge. A multi-line note also stopped reporting itself as a collision: consecutive lines overlap by design, and the guard was reserving line 1 before it measured line 2.
- **Two comments in the shipped engine described code that is not there.** Both badge passes carried a paragraph saying they consult `overlaps` and avoid symbols, notes and lozenges. They call `badgeClash` — badges against badges — and always have: the board-wide widening was built, measured at 10 HARD and three printed labels, and correctly reverted, and the prose did not go back with the code. That is worse than no comment, and it is why the C2 terminus badge sitting 5.5 mm inside St Neots' *Church Street (Market Square)* lozenge was looked for in the wrong place first. One narrow widening did survive the measurement and shipped: `drawTermBadges()` avoids an interchange lozenge, and nothing else. Three sheets on the estate have one.
- **`design.placeIndex` is in both escape-hatch gates**, so a key that stopped changing any sheet would be reported as dead code rather than quietly doing nothing. It DIFFERS on all three area sheets and on the place fixture's internal.
- **Estate effect:** printed labels 1850 → 1940, HARD 316 → 314, SOFT 244 → 237, drops 288 → 202. Eleven sheets better, three worse, and each of the three carries a ledger note: every one of their new soft defects is an index marker beside a ribbon, which the 4% ink threshold trips on a hair's breadth of overlap. The seven labels still printed over a route badge are gone. Do not read the drop number as the prize on its own — a label that got a number is identified, not placed, and the honest measure is the rendered sheet.
