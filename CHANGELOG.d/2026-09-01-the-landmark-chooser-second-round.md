date: 2026-09-01
title: "The landmark chooser went back to its reader, and two of the nine things it came back with were faults"
---

- **The *Answered* filter could not see the middle answer, and neither could anything else.** `safeSubset.js` dropped a `may` that carried no rename, on the reasoning that it "changes nothing" — and it changes nothing about the *sheet*, which is why keeping it is safe, but it is the whole of what the reader said. So *I have looked at this and it is right as it is* and *I have not reached this row yet* were one state on disk, in the count and in the filters. That is tolerable on a map somebody skims and wrong for what this screen is actually for: **145 rows, worked through by a volunteer over more than one sitting, with no way to see where they got to.** An explicit *Show if there is room* is now recorded, the page counts what has been answered, and **Not looked at yet** narrows the list to the rest.
- **A filter chip promised a fact the page did not have.** *Losing its name now* consulted no render of anything: it matched every place whose name the map is *allowed* to print, which is the exact complement of *Symbol only* sitting beside it. It now says what it does — *Named on the map*. The honest version, which POI labels the placer actually seated, can only be answered by rendering, which is what *See the real sheet* is for.
- **A save from the EDITOR page silently threw away every landmark answer.** `sanitizeOverrides()` rebuilds the overrides object from scratch, so a page that does not re-emit a key deletes it. The chooser was careful about that in its own direction from its first day — it carries the editor's route colours — and nothing had taught the editor the other half. One save there, with a success message, and a town's must / may / miss and every rename were gone. `test-landmark-tiers.mjs` now lifts the editor's two round-trip functions out of its source and runs them, because a grep for a filename proves the string is present and nothing about what it does; `prove-red-landmark-tiers.mjs` breaks that carry on purpose and watches the right assertion go red.
- **The map zooms from four gestures instead of one.** The wheel was the whole zoom, and the reader this screen is written for works on a trackpad, where a wheel gesture is not something anyone finds by accident: he reported that the map panned and could not be zoomed. There are now **+** and **−** buttons, a double-click, and `+` `−` `0` from the keyboard, all through one clamped function — and it is clamped at BOTH ends, where the wheel only ever clamped at the town, so no gesture can leave somebody looking at blank paper with nothing but *Fit* to get back.
- **The rest of the round.** A chosen row closes again on a second click or Esc, where the only way out used to be opening a different one. The list holds its place across a filter click, a search keystroke and a finished rename, anchored on the topmost visible row. The order is **A–Z by group and A–Z within it, and printed under the search box** — it was biggest-group-first with the rows inside in whatever order OpenStreetMap returned, which nothing stated and which differed between towns. The bulk buttons read `All 43:` rather than *Answer all*. The search says how many of the total it is showing. And *Copy for our records*, which copies a `poi.tiers` block for another repository, is admin-only: it is a BusMaps.uk chore and not something a local editor should have to reason about beside a button that says Save.
- **What a tier does outside the screen, asked and answered.** A *Do not show* lives in the customer's overrides, never in the delivered pack, and accepting a monthly refresh re-applies it against the UNION of what is drawn and what could be drawn — so it survives a delivery and can be switched back on. It is lost only when the place itself leaves OpenStreetMap or is renamed there, and that is reported as an obsolete override before anyone accepts. Nothing a customer ticks can move the byte gate or the quality ratchet, both of which measure the source side; exporting a town's answer into its `routes.json` is the moment the tiers reach those gates, and a batch of *Do not show* will trip the ratchet's label FLOOR, which is exactly what that floor is for.
