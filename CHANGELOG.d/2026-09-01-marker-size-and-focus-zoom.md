date: 2026-09-01
title: "A dot is a mark, not a target — and the focus zoom is now written as presses of the + button"
---

- **The plain POI disc was 26 px and is now 12.** That size was inherited rather than chosen: it is what the CSS radius it replaced happened to draw, kept because it was what the page had always shown. On a town with 171 places it is a field of overlapping circles with the streets underneath, which only became obvious once the road names gave the map something to obscure. **Shrinking the mark must not shrink the target** — clicking a place on the map is the whole of the fault fixed earlier today, and a 12 px hit area would have given it straight back with a different cause. So the mark you SEE and the target you HIT are now separate elements: a 12 px dot over a 24 px invisible disc, `fill: transparent` and not `fill: none`, because none is a hole rather than a target. Both are sized in pixels off the one stage scale, and the hit circle never shrinks below the mark, so in pictogram mode the ring is its own target.
- **The focus zoom is written as presses of the + button, because that is the only unit anybody has for it.** `FOCUS_ZOOM` was a bare `9`, and a bare 9 invites exactly the reading it got from its own author when reporting it — *"a ninth of the town"*, which sounds like area and is not. It divides the view's WIDTH, so a ninth of the width is an eighty-first of the area, and 9 was **4.67 presses** rather than the round number it looked like. It is now `ZOOM_STEP ** 3` — three presses, exactly, by construction, and it cannot drift from the buttons it is measured against. Confirmed on the live page: a click in the list and three clicks on `+` now produce the same view width to five decimal places.
- **And the animation now lands on a timer rather than on a visibility check.** The guard added with it this morning tested `document.hidden` before deciding whether to ease or jump — a **proxy** for "will frames arrive", and wrong in the direction that costs you: measured in a pane reporting `document.hidden === false` and `visibilityState === "visible"` where no `requestAnimationFrame` callback arrived in 900 ms. The guard passed, the animation never ran, and the map silently did not move. A proxy fails both ways and the silent way is the expensive one. `animateView()` now asks nothing about visibility: it schedules the landing on a plain timer and the frames, if they come, are only the nicer way of getting there. Anything that stops the animation stops the timer with it, so a hand on the map is never overruled a moment later.
