title: "The page every outreach email points at was twelve maps out of date"
---

`/opportunity.html` said **"Seven towns and five places have been built with it … with an eighth town drafted"**. That was written on 13 August and stopped being true almost immediately: counting the three folders that hold them in `buses-data` — `Areas/`, each town's `Places/`, and `Places/_standalone/` — the estate is **eight towns and twelve individual places, twenty maps**.

The sentence now says that, and says which of it is **published**: eighteen of the twenty are live here, counted off this site's own `/api/public/maps` rather than off a memory of what was delivered. The other two are High Wycombe High Street and High Wycombe Town Centre, boarding plans for a single stop, which are built to order rather than published — so the page no longer implies a reader can go and look at them.

**Built is not the same as published, and a count in prose has to say which it means.** That is the rule the rewritten CIC outreach plan states for its own numbers, and the reason this page's one sentence carried the two apart rather than quoting a single total.

**The page also gains the *why now* the outreach documents were rewritten around on 11–12 September and the page did not have.** Two statutory deliverables fall due for all **79 English local transport authorities on 31 March 2027** — a first bus network accessibility plan, consulted on and published, assessing stop infrastructure including rural stops; and every Enhanced Partnership plan updated to reflect the new measures. Separately, the Enhanced Partnership statutory guidance republished on **1 April 2026** lets a scheme require *a single set of multi-operator information available across all operators*. Both describe the artefact this system makes, and neither says whose job it is to make it.

**The paragraph deliberately quotes no "months from now".** A relative interval on a static page ages by itself with nobody editing it, which is the fault this change exists to fix. The dates are absolute and the counts are measured.
