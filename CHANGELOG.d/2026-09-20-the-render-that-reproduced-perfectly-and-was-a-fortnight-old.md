---
date: 2026-09-20
title: "A delivery is refused when the map's own manifest says the render is not the current one — the question the byte gate was never asking, and which let two of the first customer's sheets go live a fortnight stale"
---

- **`deliver-map.mjs` gains step 0b, beside the S6 gate and before the scp.** `checkNewestRender()` in `scripts/lib/newest-render.mjs` compares `path.basename(--src)` to that map's `manifest.json` `stages.S5.latest`, and a `--src` the manifest does not call current stops the delivery. Nothing is uploaded and the live service is untouched, exactly as a step-0 refusal is. Filed as buses-data OA-368 item 3.

- **What it would have caught.** On 2026-09-15 the first customer's four maps were delivered from render folders chosen with `ls -1 <map>/S5-render | tail -1`, which sorts `v2.9` after `v2.32` and `v1.9` after `v1.19`; three of the four came from renders 10, 10 and 23 builds old. The pre-flight byte gate in step 2 refused one of the three and **passed the other two**, because the engine change between those renders happens not to alter those two sheets — and both are publicly live and a fortnight out of date today. A stale render that still reproduces is, to a determinism gate, indistinguishable from a current one. The gate's silence was never evidence of anything, because reproducing was not the question.

- **It is a string equality against the recorded answer, not a version ordering.** The manifest says outright which run is current, so there is no second implementation of `v1.9 < v1.19` to get wrong — which is the fault itself, and one this project has now shipped four times. The rule was already written down in three places: this file's own header since 2026-09-01, `make-bus-leaflet/references/changing-the-engine.md`, and `prune_runs.py`'s `version_key()`. None of the three was on the path a delivery takes.

- **A refusal with an override, and the term that was supposed to decide between a refusal and a warning turns out to be unmeasurable.** OA-368 left that choice resting on how often a deliberate re-delivery of an older render has actually happened, and said the number was one read-only query away on the live host. It is not: nothing on the host records which render a delivery came from. `map_version.storage_key` is the portal's own `v1.0`, hard-coded by `import-map.mjs`, and the audit-log `src` written at fulfilment is the container's `/import`, because `deliver-map.mjs` rewrites `--src` before the importer sees it. Nobody can count it, so the instrument is the one that is safe under either answer: it stops, and it prints `--render-superseded "<reason>"`, the same bargain `--s6-unchecked` already strikes in that file.

- **Every way of not knowing is its own verdict and none of them passes quietly.** No manifest above the render, an unparseable one, and a manifest naming no current S5 run are each `cannot-tell` and each refuse — "could not check" and "checked and fine" reporting alike is the failure the S6 gate beside this one exists for. A current render that is **not on this disk** still supersedes, and says so: `S5-render` is gitignored and `prune_runs.py` deletes old runs, so a tree can record a render it no longer holds, and that is a reason to go and fetch it rather than to ship the older one. The one case that passes in silence is a `--src` that is not a versioned render folder at all — a committed `_portal-fixture` pack, about which no manifest claims anything.

- **Proved able to fail, and the control is the delivery that was right.** `scripts/prove-red-newest-render.mjs` runs seven arms with the unmutated copy green: the text sort restored verbatim, `cannot-tell` folded into a pass, the not-on-disk case read as "nothing newer", and four on the wiring — the predicate uncalled, the refusal downgraded to a warning, the override forwarded to an importer that would reject it, and the gate moved after the scp. Arm 1 must **not** redden St Neots (area), the one of the four 2026-09-15 deliveries a text sort got right; an arm that broke every case would say nothing about the sort. Two of the seven found real weaknesses while being written — arm 1's control was originally the wrong case, and the ordering assertion was being satisfied by `function gateNewestRender()` rather than by the call.
