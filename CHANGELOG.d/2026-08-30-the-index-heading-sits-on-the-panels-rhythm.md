date: 2026-08-30
title: "The index heading sits on the panel's own rhythm"
---

- **"Numbered on the map" was 1.8 mm above its first entry, against a 3.4 mm pitch between the entries themselves — half — so the heading read as the first item of its own list.** Found by Peter on the shipped sheets, hours after the numbered place index went out. `services_panel.js` has had the right formula since the type scale landed: `gapDown(from, air, rise) = from × DESC + air + rise`, the baseline-to-baseline distance leaving `air` mm of clear space between one line's descenders and the topmost ink of the next. It is what puts *Services* above its first badge and *Key* above its first pictogram. The index heading used one term of it — `SZ × CAP`, the entry's cap-height, with the heading's own descender and all 3.2 mm of air dropped. The correct figure is 5.71. The gap above the heading was 6.0 against the rhythm's 8.06, so it did not match the *Key* heading immediately above it either.
- **The panel now hands the formula out instead of the caller re-deriving it.** `drawServicesPanel` returns `{x, x1, endY, rhythm}`, and what can rot silently after that is the export rather than the arithmetic: a `rhythm` that is not the one the panel uses itself would put the index back on a spacing of its own with every byte gate green, because the panel's own ink would not have moved. Three tests pin it, one of them stating the rule rather than the number — *a heading must sit further from its first row than the rows sit from each other* — and two mutations prove they bite.
- **The first fix was wrong in a way no amount of arithmetic could show.** `gapDown` returns a baseline-to-baseline distance and it was applied from the top of the heading's box, whose baseline is `HEADSZ` lower — so the gap became 2.31 mm, barely more than the 1.8 it replaced, with every number in the expression correct. A 300 dpi crop of the panel foot is what caught it. Every `y` in that block is now measured from the heading's baseline.
- **Cost, measured rather than estimated: one index entry each on High Wycombe's diagram and schematic.** The block is 6 mm taller, and those are the only two sheets on the board whose index had no spare row. They are also the two that could not number about fifty names in the first place, so what is lost is the fifty-first thing those sheets do not say — and both ledger rows now record the trade. Every other sheet keeps every entry it had; board totals are labels 1940 → 1938, drop 202 → 204, HARD and SOFT unchanged.
- **A byte count is not a diff, and this round produced a clean example.** St Ives' internal sheet is 522,952 bytes before the change and 522,952 after. The heading moved from `y=173.12` to `171.78` and its entry from `174.92` to `177.49`; those numbers happen to have the same digit count. Anything comparing sizes would have reported the fixture as already current.
