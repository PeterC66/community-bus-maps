date: 2026-09-13
title: "Four pages told you to put the sheet on a noticeboard and none said how high or whose wall it was"
---

**The site now says where to put a printed sheet, whose permission to ask, and what to print it on.** A new FAQ entry, *We're putting one up in a bus shelter — how high, and do we need permission?*, sits beside *Can I print them?* and carries all three; the accessibility statement gains a standalone section, *If you put a printed sheet up*; and the customer guide points at the FAQ from the paragraph about downloading print files. **`terms.html` and `legal.html` are deliberately untouched** — this is advice, and advice on a licence page reads as a condition.

**The gap was found in the field, and it could not have been found here.** Two readers said publicly that they could not read our St Neots sheet in a bus shelter at Love's Farm because it was mounted above wheelchair and above eye height; a third pointed out it sat above the head of the person who put it up; and a fourth asked whether anybody had permission to fix it there. Four documents — `terms.html`, `legal.html`, the FAQ and the customer guide — invite a customer to put the sheet on a noticeboard, and all four attach the same two conditions (do not alter it, do not crop the credits) and the same live-times warning. **Not one of them mentioned height, reach or whose wall it was.** A page's height above the ground is not a property anything in this repository can observe, so no amount of WCAG work could have surfaced it; the only instrument that found it was somebody standing at the stop.

**The numbers are the Department for Transport's, not ours, and the citation is the part to get right.** [Inclusive Mobility](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/inclusive-mobility-making-transport-accessible-for-passengers-and-pedestrians) (2022): a detailed sign centred about 1400mm with the whole of it between 900mm and 1800mm, and — where there is room — **duplicated**, one copy centred 1600–1700mm and another at 1000–1100mm. That duplication is an expensive recommendation for a council and a free one for our customers, because printing our sheets costs nothing to license, which is why it is in the entry rather than left out as impractical. **The edition a search returns first is the 2005 one, withdrawn on 10 January 2022, and it is the only version that exists as readable HTML** — both editions give the same figures, so a dead citation would carry a live number and nobody checking the number would catch it.

**Height is load-bearing for the accessible alternative, which is why it is in the accessibility statement and not only the FAQ.** The QR code on every sheet sits 2–22mm above its bottom edge and leads to the map's service list — the text alternative every other claim in that statement leans on. A sheet mounted out of reach does not merely become hard to read; it severs the only route the paper has to its own accessible version.

**Advice, not a licence condition, and the reason is not softness.** We cannot observe how anybody mounts a sheet, so a condition could never fire; and a condition would imply we are responsible for the display, contradicting what the same entry says about permission over somebody else's property being the organisation's to obtain. The existing conditions are properties of the artefact and visible in any copy; a height is a property of a wall.

**On the law the entry states a definition and refuses to apply it.** Fly-posting means a display put up without the consent of the owner or occupier, so consent is what separates the two cases and asking is what settles it — and whether any particular sheet in any particular shelter is or is not fly-posting is a question about that structure and that authority, which we do not answer. The entry tells a reader to ask the owner and to ask at the same time whether anything further is needed. A bus shelter is usually the town or parish council's, sometimes the district council's, and sometimes an advertising contractor's; in Cambridgeshire, where most of the estate is, the county council states in writing that shelters are **not** part of the highway asset it manages, so the obvious guess is the wrong one.

**No engine change, and that was ruled out rather than overlooked.** The QR sits at the bottom of the sheet, which is exactly where the same guidance wants information of particular relevance to wheelchair users. The code is out of reach because the sheet is mounted high, not because the artwork is wrong. The fix is a sentence.
