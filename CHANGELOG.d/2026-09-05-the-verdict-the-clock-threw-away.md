title: "The verdict the clock threw away, and which two commits a verify run is about"
---

buses-data OA-243 and the portal half of OA-018, one CI bookkeeping change each.

- **`audit` has a 20-minute budget, not 10.** On 2026-09-04 the registry, or the runners' egress to it, slowed by two orders of magnitude with no package file touched — `npm ci` from seconds to minutes, `npm audit --omit=dev` from one second to four or five — and this job was killed **three times in a day**, every time *after* its check had printed `OK - nothing new, and no deferral has expired.` PR #223's pass printed at 10:12:34.435 and the job was cancelled at 10:12:34.452. Its three siblings absorbed the same loss because their budgets are 15, 30 and 20; `audit` could not, because both of its steps are the slow ones. The slowness recovered by itself the same afternoon — attempt 2 of the third kill took eight seconds, and every run since takes 11–18 s — so 20 is a budget for a bad afternoon, not a new normal. If the job regularly takes minutes, the real question is why it makes two round trips to the registry when `npm ci` already resolves against the committed lockfile. A gate red for a reason unrelated to what it measures teaches people to re-run without reading, and a run listing shows only the last attempt, so the three kills are invisible behind three green re-runs; ask `.../runs/{id}/attempts/1`.
- **`verify` now says which two commits its verdict is about.** The portal is checked out at the triggering commit; the fixtures come from buses-data at whatever its `main` holds the minute the job runs, because that checkout names no `ref:`. A new step prints both SHAs into the log and the step summary, mirroring the step buses-data's `gates.yml` gained on 2026-08-28. *Report, don't pin*: pinning the fixtures would make the gate reproducible and blind to the drift it exists to catch. What it buys is that a red can be diffed against a known-good pairing rather than a known-good Tuesday — PR #107's truthful red of 2026-08-27, a v1.14 reference measured against a v1.15 engine, was exactly the case nothing could name after the fact.
