- **Four generators re-vendored from `claude-skills` `c0c6dd1`, and all 20 maps re-rolled on the data side.** `labeller.js`, `label_placer.js`, `area/gen_external_radial.js` and `place/gen_internal.js`. `npm run verify:place` PASS on 4 sheets across 2 fixtures; `npm run track:engine --apply` brought 8 local packs forward, 3 already current, 2 skipped. **And it was then run on the live store**, which a merge does not do — `track-engine.mjs` reads `data/maps`, which is gitignored, so the packs the public renders from carry their own copy of the generator. Deployed as `320a752` (`/health?deep=1`: `gitSha=320a752`, `builtAt=2026-08-30T12:56:46Z`, every check ok), then **18 packs behind, 18 brought forward, 20 already current, 8 skipped**. Read back by re-running the REPORTING form, which exits non-zero when anything is behind: **38 current, 0 behind, exit 0**. Stored sheets are untouched by design; what changes is the next render of each map. The 8 skipped are OA-143 in the flesh — an area pack records `gen_external.js` without saying which of the two external generators drew it — and every round will keep skipping them until that provenance is recorded. It is still a hand step, as it was on 2026-08-28.
- **Three reservations in the engine were computed from a character-count guess while the glyphs were drawn from real Arial metrics.** The interchange label reserved to `x+24` whatever the town's interchange is called: Beaconsfield's *Old Town (Aylesbury End / The Saracen's Head)* draws 71.5 mm against 26 reserved — 45 mm of a 297 mm page treated as empty — and High Wycombe 41.4, St Neots 46.4, Huntingdon 37.4. The road-name placer guessed `length × 2.5 × 0.5`, putting *Ramsey Road* at 13.75 mm against 15.84 drawn. Map notes did the same. Every one of those gaps was space a later pass believed was blank and put a label in, which is why five sheets carried label-on-label collisions and now carry none.
- **Measuring the road-name box properly cost St Ives its *Ramsey Road* and *Somersham Road* outright on the first dry run**, because that pass drops a name when its seven candidates are blocked rather than looking further. It now tries every segment midpoint after those seven, in the order it already sorts them into. Fifteen road names that were being dropped in silence are printed for the first time across the estate.
- **A map note and a station lozenge were drawn at their hand-authored position and reserved afterwards**, so nothing before them knew they were there and nothing after them could help. They now claim in the claim phase, beside the icons — the same move the labeller makes for a `fixed` label — and when the space was already taken they say so on stderr, naming what they hit. Page chrome is deliberately excluded, because High Wycombe draws four notes inside the services panel column on purpose and a warning that named the panel would have been wrong on its first run. The first thing it found that was not chrome: two High Wycombe notes drawn across the *River Wye* feature label.
- **A leader line starts at its symbol's rim instead of its centre.** Found from outside the project, at magnification, on the Ramsey sheet: the leader begins at the exact centre of the badge disc and labels are painted last, so it crosses the digit. Measured there — `r=3.0` discs at `x=158.22`, leaders 5.01 mm long — three fifths of each leader was drawn on the badge it came out of.
- **`prefer` is honoured by the labeller at last, and is off by default, and the second half is the finding.** The radial generator computed a preferred side for each spoke and the labeller read sixteen item properties without it, so 81 of the board's 83 spokes carried a `side` that had done nothing since labels engine v2 became the default. The mechanism is fixed, tested and mutation-proved, and gated behind `design.labelPrefer` because turning the board on at once was measured: it improved no sheet's numbers and moved one of Ramsey's four *Bury* labels from 31.9 mm to 23.9 mm of another, turning a clean external sheet into a HARD defect. Ramsey states `side:"up"` on all six of its branches, which is what a config looks like when nobody has ever seen its effect. Every external sheet on the estate is byte-identical except for its version stamp.
- **Built, measured and NOT shipped: badges avoiding symbols.** Widening the two forcing badge passes' obstacle set was implemented twice — once against everything reserved, once narrowed to map ink alone — and board-wide it cost 10 HARD and three printed labels, seven of them on High Wycombe internal, to remove a handful of visible overlaps. Widen the obstacle set on a sheet that is mostly obstacles and the placer drops rather than moves.
- **And the preview tool could not have told anybody that.** `preview_design.js` copies `routes.json` out with the sheets, and its own header explains at length why — without it every ink measure reads far too low, and a scratch build once reported labels-over-ink collapsing from 13 to 0, "which was pure fiction". The drop count is read the same way, from `unplaced*.json` beside the SVG, and that was not being copied, so every preview sheet answered `unplacedLabels: 0`. Measured on Wisbech internal: shipped 7 dropped and hard 8, preview 0 and 0. The `DEF` column it prints was never wrong, because `defects` excludes drops on both sides — which is exactly why it survived. The number the tool printed was fair; every number computed from the tree it left behind was not.
- **Estate effect, measured with the sidecars present:** printed labels 1829 → 1850, HARD 322 → 316, SOFT 247 → 244, drops 286 → 288. Eighteen sheets better, nine worse, and each of the nine carries its own ledger note naming what it traded. Labels printed over a route badge went 49/36/13 to 44/37/7; the seven that remain are all hand-authored positions the engine now names at build time.
