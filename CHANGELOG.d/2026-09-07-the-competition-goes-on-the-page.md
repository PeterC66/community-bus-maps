title: "The competition goes on the page: a market-town sheet is £970 on the public record, and the update is where the money goes"
---

Two public pages, for buses-data OA-264, after OA-263 found the answer to a question the St Ives booklet's co-designer asked in August: does a commercial company already do this?

- **`opportunity.html`, the opening card.** It promised maps *at a small fraction of what a design agency charges*. On the public record a market-town schematic is £970, so that was not true of the sheet; it is true of the update. The sentence now says an agency can draw the first edition for a few hundred pounds, and that what it charges by the hour for every edition after is the thing this takes out.
- **`opportunity.html`, a fourth card under *What is not resolved*: `#who-else`.** Because a serious candidate will find Contracts Finder before they find us. Three procurement notices with their values (West Yorkshire 2017, West Northamptonshire 2024, Transport for Wales 2026), the itemised £970 and £88-an-hour figures from the West Northamptonshire estimate, the fact that nobody in that trade generates the sheet from the open-data feed or sells to a parish, and the county poster the Cambridgeshire & Peterborough Combined Authority already owns, on which each town is a node. Every figure is a public procurement record, linked, and the card cites nothing internal.
- **`pricing.html`, *Against a design agency*.** The unquantified *order of magnitude below* is gone, replaced by the £970 and £6,600 figures and the intention they support: price the first sheet below an agency's and cover the updates, because the update is where the money goes. Links to `#who-else` for the sources.
- **`pricing.html`, *The honest version*.** It said the alternative to us *is nothing*, for everyone. It is nothing for a parish or a bus users' group, because the agencies sell to authorities in £50,000 programmes and not to a town; for a council the alternative is real and priced, and it sells an edition rather than a map that stays right. The paragraph now says both.
