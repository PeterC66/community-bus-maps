---
date: 2026-08-29
title: "The boarding index reaches the services page"
---

- **`/m/<slug>/services` now carries the "Where to board" index, which is the boarding sheet's own accessibility argument and the largest remaining piece of step 3 (OA-010).** `boarding_index.json` has travelled with every boarding payload since 2026-08-23 and nothing read it. A boarding index is inherently a table — destination, the stop you stand at, the walk to it, the services that go there — so this is the one output whose text equivalent is arguably *better* than the picture rather than a consolation prize, and it is the point on which a PDF-only spider map cannot compete. On High Wycombe High Street the section is 6 stands, 54 destinations and 12.8 KB of real markup.
- **The model drops everything internal at `buildFacts()`, not at the public read model.** No ATCO codes, no lat/lon, and none of the `trips` / `arrivalM` / `arrivalBand` numbers that decided which stand a destination was assigned to. A reader standing at a bus stop needs the letter on the flag, not the arithmetic behind it — and three assertions hold that line, each watched go red on a deliberate leak.
- **A stand with nothing boarded at it is printed, in words, rather than dropped.** "Stop S — High Street · facing west · 1 min walk (31 m) · no bus on this sheet is boarded here". Three of High Wycombe High Street's six stands are in that state; a reader standing at one of them is exactly who needs telling.
- **The sheet's own caveats travel with the table.** `boardingPlan.note[]` says which journeys are *not* indexed (school and works workings, the WW1 shuttle, the town-only services). Without them the text version would claim a completeness the picture explicitly disclaims.
- **A version published before today has no boarding index in its facts snapshot, so `factsForPublicMap()` backfills that one field from the live payload — and only for a snapshot at `schema: 1`.** Re-rendering an already-published map is a portal operation that needs a session, and the maps in question are precisely the ones whose accessibility case *is* the table. Every later render writes its own, at which point the backfill never runs again for that version. The rule holds everywhere else: the snapshot describes the picture that was signed off.
- **`.muted` is not a rule in this stylesheet.** The grey on that page comes from `.card p` and `.card ul`, so a `<div class="muted small">` inside a table cell would have rendered as ordinary body text at 0.85 rem. The "also from" line has its own class instead. Found by looking at the rendered page, not at the markup.
