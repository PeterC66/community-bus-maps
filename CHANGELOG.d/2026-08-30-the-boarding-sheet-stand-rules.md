date: 2026-08-30
title: "A boarding sheet stops sending readers to the wrong flag"
---

- **The stand a boarding sheet names is now chosen on service as well as distance, and one row changes what it tells a reader entirely.** The picker sorted on the printed walking minute first, so a nearer stand beat a better-served one however lopsided the trade. High Wycombe Town Centre was offering Marlow from Bay 15 — **five journeys a week, marked as a limited service** — while Bay 9, one printed minute further, runs it **317 times a week**. The sheet now prints the 850 from Bay 9 and the limited-service asterisk is correctly gone. Amersham and Chesham on the High Street sheet move the same way, from 72 and 66 journeys a week to 418 and 352.
- **The guard that stops the same rule going wrong is the one worth naming.** Three St Ives destinations — Fen Drayton, Longstanton and Swavesey — have exactly the shape the promotion looks for, and must not flip: the better-served option there is the Busway, which sets a passenger down between 800 m and 1.4 km outside the village they asked for. A promoted stand has to get **no further from the destination**, not merely carry more buses, so St Ives is untouched.
- **"You are already there" is now a question about containment rather than a 30-metre constant.** The old rule split High Wycombe's Stop R (9 m) from Stop S (31 m) by one metre, on a sheet that prints "1 min" for both. Keying it on the printed minute instead — the obvious repair — was built and **rejected on measurement**: one minute reaches 120 m, which at St Ives Bus Station takes in The Busway Station Road at 47 m, a stop that is not in the bus station. The caption asserts containment, so it tests containment against the locator's own polygons. Byte-identical at St Ives.
- **A flag that prints a compass word is now a usable boarding instruction.** Two stops both called "Mill Road", one printed "N-bound" and the other "S-bound", used to refuse the whole sheet. Measured over the NaPTAN register: of 81,050 code-less stops sharing a name with a neighbour within 250 m, **7,287 sit in a cluster the compass word separates completely**. The 1,771 in a cluster only *some* of whose flags carry a word stay refused, because a reader sent to the unmarked flag has been given a name that matches all of them.
- **The checker was widened to accept a walk that bought something, not taught the generator's answer.** Left alone, its "no longer walk than needed" note fired on all three promotions the day they shipped — correct behaviour reported as a defect, which is how a soft note gets muted within a week. It now counts the trips **itself, from the feed**, rather than reading the index it is checking, so the thing under test cannot grant its own acceptance.
