title: "Where we stand on politics — written once, so it never has to be argued case by case"
---

Documentation only, for buses-data OA-261 item 1. The site said nothing about whether an organisation's politics bears on whether it can publish a map, and the question had already arrived in practice: one of our own pilot sheets appeared, printed and pinned up in a bus shelter, in a video that ends on a political party's card. Nothing about that breached anything — the sheet was compliant, watermark, credits and pilot band all visible — and that is exactly why the answer belongs on a page rather than in a reply.

- **`faq.html`, a new entry, `#non-partisan`.** *Is this political? Can a political party publish a map?* Yes — a party, a campaign group or a councillor's office is an organisation like any other, we decline none for its politics and favour none. The entry then says why that is workable rather than merely generous: a sheet is drawn from BODS and OpenStreetMap by ordinary code, shows the services that exist, carries the publishing organisation's name and badge and nobody else's, and has no editorial line in it to capture.
- **The two conditions are restated as conditions that already existed.** Credits stay on, the sheet is not altered — [`legal.html`](https://busmaps.uk/legal.html) has required both since before this question came up, because the open data licences make the attribution travel with the map. So a party logo added to a printed sheet is an alteration we do not allow, and no new rule had to be invented to say so.
- **It states its own limit instead of implying there isn't one.** A published sheet is a public download; anyone may print one and display it where they like and we have no say in that. What we can do is keep a reader able to see whose map it is, which is what the credits and the organisation's badge are for. Claiming any more control than that would have been untrue.
- **`Who can apply?` gains a pointer**, so the reader who is actually wondering meets the answer where the question occurs to them.
- **`background.html`** gains it as a fourth item in *what holds this together*, beside the open data, the operator cross-check and the source-available licence — the list was three items and is now four.

**Why now, and not after something goes wrong.** A neutrality position produced in response to a particular case reads as a judgement on that case. Written in advance it is a rule, and the next organisation to ask can be pointed at a page instead of being argued with.
