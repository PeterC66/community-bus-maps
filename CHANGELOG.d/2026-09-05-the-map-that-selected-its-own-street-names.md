title: "The map that selected its own street names, and the filter that was empty for ever"
---

buses-data OA-252. Two faults on the landmark chooser, reported together by the reader the screen is written for, and only the first is a bug.

- **A double-click zoomed the map and highlighted the street underneath it.** `wireMapGestures()` calls `e.preventDefault()` on `dblclick` to stop exactly that, and **that call cannot do it**: Chrome makes the word selection on the second `mousedown`, before a `dblclick` event exists to prevent. So the gesture did both, and a second double-click cleared the highlight — which is what was reported. Fixed with `user-select: none` on `.lm-stage svg`; nothing in that stage is text anyone would want to select.
- **Measured in a browser, both ways, twice.** First in a throwaway page holding two identical SVGs double-clicked by the browser itself rather than by a synthetic event: without the rule `getSelection()` read `"Needingworth "`, with it `""`. Then on the real page with the real stylesheet, with the rule toggled off and on as its own control — off, a double-click selected all three road names; on, it selected nothing, and still zoomed. The probe also found the half nobody had reported: **a pan drag selected `"amsey Road\nNeedingworth Road"`**, so the map's primary gesture was painting the streets blue as well. That is fixed by the same line.
- **"Not looked at yet" is empty for the whole of a finished map's life, and now says so.** The chip filters on the server's `answered` flag, which is true for any key named in either tier layer — so once the reader has been through the list and pressed Save nothing is unanswered, and it stays that way across the export into `routes.json` and across a rebuild. The chip said *Nothing matches that*, which reads as a broken control. It is not spent: it is where a place OpenStreetMap adds later arrives, and the only thing in the system that mentions one. The count now sits on the button (`Not looked at yet 0`, `Answered 145`) so the answer is given without pressing it, and the empty state under it is the sentence explaining the filter's second life.
- **A returning reader is told the number, and told it honestly.** A part-answered map shows a line naming how many rows have no answer and where they are — and does **not** claim they are new, because the page cannot tell: the export only carries rows that were answered, so an unfinished map's leftovers survive a build looking exactly like an arrival.
- **One count was wrong and is now one predicate.** `onEdit()` counted answered rows over `STATE`, a Map keyed on the POI key, against a denominator of `LANDMARKS.length` — so on a town with two unnamed pharmacies both keyed `pharmacy:` a fully answered map reported *144 of 145*. The tally, the two chips and the list they filter now all ask `isAnswered()`.
