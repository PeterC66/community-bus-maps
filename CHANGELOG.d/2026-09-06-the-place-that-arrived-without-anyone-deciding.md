buses-data OA-253, the cheap end of the two options, chosen by Peter on 2026-09-06.

- **A landmark OpenStreetMap gains between two builds could print on the refreshed sheet with nothing anywhere mentioning it.** It enters the candidate list answered by neither tier layer, so it takes the default — *show if there is room* — and the monthly update described a sheet that had a new symbol on it without describing the symbol. Three things could have said so and only one did: the landmark chooser's *Not looked at yet* chip. `changeSummary()` compares two versions' **overrides**, so it reports a landmark a person promoted and cannot report one that simply arrived; and the generator's `poi.tiers:` warnings reach `/preview` and `/save`, which is the editing path, not the accept-an-update path.
- **`dataChangeSummary()` now diffs the candidate sets too**, and `diffLandmarks()` is the pure half over two candidate lists. Keyed on the POI key, `<cat>:<name>`, because that is the identity a tier answer is written against — so "appeared" means precisely *arrived carrying no answer, and will therefore be drawn if there is room*. A rename reads as one arrival and one departure, which is the truthful answer rather than a limitation: the answer really is orphaned.
- **It falls SILENT when it is not sure, which is the opposite of this estate's usual rule and is deliberate.** `enumerateCandidatesFromDir()` returns `[]` for a folder with no `osm.json`, for a payload whose selector will not load, and for a place pack carrying neither — and an empty list on one side alone would report the whole of the other side as arrivals. "145 new places" on a refresh that changed nothing is worse than the silence it replaced, and the silence it replaced is exactly what stood before. A side with no candidates suppresses the comparison and says so in `landmarksKnown`, which `propose-update.mjs` prints rather than leaving as an absence.
- **`unchanged` moves with it.** `isEmptyDataChange()` returns `unchanged` verbatim when present, so a refresh whose only change was a new landmark would otherwise go on reporting itself as identical on every screen. A summary stored before this existed keeps its own verdict; nothing is retro-computed.
- **One sentence, two screens.** The wording lived in `editor.js` for about an hour before being folded into `changes.js`, which both the customer's accept screen and the approver's review already load. `chooserHref` is the difference: the customer's bullet says what happens if they do nothing and links to the chooser, the approver's states the fact. A duplicate is how two screens come to describe one refresh differently, and it puts half the sentence outside the only test that reads it.
- **The count is the sentence and the names are the aside**, capped at six. Real landmark churn was **never measurable on this laptop** — every pair of payloads here carries the same OSM pull, so two attempts at measuring it returned zero for reasons that had nothing to do with OSM — so the line is built to survive a noisy answer rather than assuming a quiet one.
- **Nine mutations, each caught by the assertion that names it.** `scripts/test-landmark-arrivals.mjs` and `scripts/prove-red-landmark-arrivals.mjs`; the suite ends on the JOIN every other assertion takes on trust — that the editor page loads `changes.js`, calls `landmarkBullets`, passes its own map's chooser link, and keeps no second copy of the wording. The harness's own control earned its keep on the day: `views/` was missing from the scratch tree and an intact copy failed with ENOENT instead of every mutation quietly passing.
