- **`LICENSING.md` still recorded CSRF as an accepted risk three days after it was fixed, and that is the one that mattered.** The row read *"**Accepted risk for the pilot** — deferred, not fixed"*, with the reasoning that `SameSite=Lax` covers cross-site POST and the residual same-site risk was judged low for a handful of known pilot users. CSRF landed on 2026-08-25 in `8787a72`. A security-posture table is read precisely when somebody wants to know what is *not* protected, so a stale row there does not merely mislead — it invites a decision on the strength of a risk that no longer exists. Now marked fixed, with the mechanism named and **verified live rather than merely merged**: `curl -sI https://busmaps.uk/` returns `Set-Cookie: cbm_csrf=…; SameSite=Lax; Secure` on `0.10.0-pilot+2eec3ac`.
- **`ROADMAP.md` carried the same claim in the other register** — a to-do bullet saying `SameSite=Lax` covers it "for now" — sitting among a dozen neighbours already marked ✅ Done. Two documents making one stale claim in two registers is why this was worth sweeping for rather than fixing when noticed. It also closes `OA-093` in the buses-data backlog, which existed only because these two documents said the work was outstanding.
- **Three runbooks called R6 "planned" while `R6-incident-response.md` sat in the same folder**, written, stamped v1.2 and dated 7 August. `R2-onboarding.md`, `R3-review-and-publish.md` and `R5-marketing-and-messages.md` each route the reader to incident response at the moment they most need it — a suspension prompted by a problem, a wrong map published, a complaint about a live sheet — and each told them the runbook did not exist yet. All three now link it.
- **`H2-todo-quickstart.md` said the same unproven thing twice, eight days apart, and neither copy was obliged to notice the other.** A note dated 10 August and a bullet dated 18 August both recorded that brand-new-map delivery is not proven end to end against a real request. That claim is *still true* — re-checked on 28 August and nothing had settled it — so this is not a correction but a consolidation: the bullet is now the single record, the note points at it, and the bullet says what would settle it (one delivery against a real approved request, `maprequest.fulfil` in the audit log, the map live). An unproven path quietly reads as a proven one the longer an undated sentence sits unchanged.
