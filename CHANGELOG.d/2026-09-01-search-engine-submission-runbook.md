date: 2026-09-01
title: "The search-engine submission gets a runbook, and a pre-flight whose pass is a positive statement"
---

- **R5 gains a *Search engines — verification and the sitemap* section** (`docs/R5-marketing-and-messages.md#search-engines--verification-and-the-sitemap`; written as a path rather than a link because a fragment is read both here and inlined into `CHANGELOG.md` at the repository root, and no one relative path is correct in both places). Indexing was opened on 2026-08-21 and nothing since has told a search engine the site exists; the submission has sat as an open action largely because it is **an account action in somebody's browser** — a signed-in Google account, a signed-in Bing account and a DNS `TXT` record at 20i — with no script that can perform it and no check that can confirm it. The runbook says that plainly rather than leaving the next reader to rediscover it, and then writes down the three decisions that are easy to get wrong: a **Domain** property rather than URL-prefix, the record going in at `@` on 20i and **staying there for ever** because Google re-checks and silently un-verifies, and Bing's *Import from Google Search Console* as the reason to do Google first.
- **The half that is checkable has four commands, and all four were run against production on 2026-09-01.** `robots.txt` carries no `Disallow: /`, so `ALLOW_INDEXING` is genuinely on. `sitemap.xml` holds **48 distinct URLs — thirteen static pages, seventeen maps, seventeen `/services` pages and the pilot organisation**, all answering `200` directly with no redirect, no 404 and no `noindex`. `www.busmaps.uk` now answers **`308`** to the apex, preserving path and query, and all thirteen shopfront pages carry a self-referential canonical — the two faults this pre-flight found when it was first written, both since fixed and deployed, both re-read off the live site here rather than carried forward on the strength of the entry that fixed them.
- **Command 3 no longer passes by printing nothing.** It used to end `| grep -v '^200 '` and report a clean sitemap as an absence of output — but a `while read` loop that consumed no input prints exactly the same nothing, so the check could not distinguish a healthy site from one where the URL list never reached it. It now tallies the status codes (`| sort | uniq -c`) and the pass is the single line `48 200`, a positive statement carrying its own population, which the reader is told to compare against the count command 2 just printed. Falsified rather than assumed: the new form goes red on a 404, red on a redirect, and — the case that motivated it — **prints nothing on empty input where the old form printed nothing and meant pass**, all three measured before this landed.
- **The count is now written with its breakdown.** The figure recorded on 2026-08-29 was 47 and the parts listed beside it summed to 45; neither number was questioned for three days, because a total on its own has nothing to be checked against. The runbook now asks for the breakdown every time the figure is re-recorded, and the current one adds up.
