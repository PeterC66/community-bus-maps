---
date: 2026-08-31
title: "A seeded council that read as real"
---

- **The pending-applications rollup no longer mixes real applicants with seed data, because it could only ever be reported as wholly one or the other.** `buildWorklist` emitted a single item — *"Decide 8 organisation applications"* — covering every pending record, and on the dev checkout those eight are seven obvious `Test <sector>` rows from `seed-demo.mjs` plus one called *"Ramsey Town Council"*, which reads exactly like a real council. On 2026-08-31 that row was read from the operator's laptop, the one plausible name among seven implausible ones was taken as genuine, and Peter was told a council was waiting on his decision. He replied that he did not think Ramsey Town Council had applied, which was correct: `seed-demo.mjs:169` creates it too. The queue now splits into a real item (`applications`) and a seeded one (`applications-demo`, carrying `demo: true`), and if either half is empty its row simply does not exist.
- **The test is the address, not the name, and that is the whole reason it can be trusted.** RFC 2606 reserves `example.com/.net/.org` and the `.example`, `.test` and `.invalid` TLDs; RFC 6761 adds `.localhost`. They resolve nowhere and accept no mail, which is why fixtures use them — and which makes "this applicant cannot be reached" a fact about the address rather than a naming convention somebody has to remember to follow. The seeded council is `clerk@ramsey-tc.example`; the seven `Test` rows share `t@example.com`. Exported as `isUnreachableAddress` and asserted directly against the near-misses that would make it dangerous: `notexample.com`, `example.co.uk`, `testing.com` and `example.company` are all real, and a trailing-dot FQDN is not.
- **The existing suite's only application fixture was `clerk@example.invalid`,** so every assertion about the applications item would have silently moved onto the demo path while still reading as though it described the real one. It is now `clerk@elsewhere-tc.gov.uk`, and the seeded shapes are added beside it so the split is exercised rather than assumed.
- **Nineteen new checks, proved red before being trusted.** Reverting the split turns eight of them red, and the one worth reading prints the fault verbatim: `Waiting: Elsewhere Town Council, Ramsey Town Council, Test council` — real and seeded in one sentence, which is the sentence that misled a reader. The first attempt at that proof reported a single failure and stopped, because the next assertion dereferenced an item that no longer existed; the assertions are optional-chained now, so a regression reports its whole extent instead of its first symptom.
