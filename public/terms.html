Terms of use

Short version: the service is free, the maps are a carefully-checked guide rather than live times, you keep the credits on the sheets, and you only make maps for places you have a genuine reason to represent.

Working draft — last reviewed 25 July 2026; to be confirmed before the service opens publicly. The system is currently a pilot with no customers. This is a plain-English starting point, not legal advice.

Who this is for

These terms are between BusMaps.uk (“we”, “us”) and an approved organisation and the people it invites to use the service on its behalf (“you”). They apply once your application is approved and you have an account.

The service

It’s free, and community-run

There is no charge and we take no payment details. It is a small community project, offered as it is, and we may change, pause or withdraw parts of it — we’ll try to give reasonable notice for anything that affects a published map.

What you can do

Within your agreed quota you can request area or place maps, and for a built map you can recolour routes, toggle landmarks on or off, choose which of the standard outputs it produces, save versions, and send a version to us for review. Bigger changes (geometry, layout, and the hand-finished tube-map diagram, which is asked for rather than switched on) are made centrally by us.

Your side of the deal

Represent only what you may

Ask for maps only for an area or place you have a genuine reason to represent — your parish, your shop, your school, your event. Don’t imply that a bus operator, a council or anyone else endorses your map when they don’t.

Look after your access

We sign you in with a one-time link to your email rather than a password. Keep your email account secure, and don’t share your sign-in links. Tell us if you think someone else has got into your account.

Keep it accurate and fair

Give us correct details, and use the editor for its purpose. Don’t try to get around the limits on what can be edited, overload the service, or use it to mislead.

Get it checked before it’s public

Only a version a reviewer has approved becomes the published, public map. Don’t present an unpublished draft as the official map, and don’t alter a downloaded sheet in a way that changes the service information or removes the credits.

Accuracy — and the honest limit of a printed map

Every map is reviewed by a person before it is published — a check that it looks right, not an independent verification against timetables. Bus services change regardless, and a printed sheet can’t show today’s times. So the maps are a guide to which buses go where, not live departure information — every sheet says to check times with the operator or at bustimes.org, and you should keep that message with the map. If you spot something wrong, tell us from that map’s page and we’ll look into it.

Using and sharing the maps

Published sheets are meant to be used: print them, put them on a noticeboard, include them in a newsletter or a community leaflet. The maps are built from OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL) and UK bus open data via BODS (Open Government Licence); keep the credits that appear on the sheet. See our privacy & licensing page for the detail.

Your data

What we hold and why — and how to ask for a copy, a correction or deletion — is set out in the privacy & licensing notice. In short: only what we need to run the service, no tracking, no selling of your details.

Suspending or ending

You can stop using the service at any time — tell us and we’ll close your account. We may suspend or withdraw a map or an account if these terms are broken, if a map raises a safety or accuracy concern we can’t quickly resolve, or if we have to stop offering the service. Where we withdraw a published map we’ll say why.
