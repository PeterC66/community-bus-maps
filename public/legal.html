Privacy, licensing & attribution

Short version: we hold only what we need to run the service, we set no tracking cookies, and the maps are built from open data that must be credited.

Working draft — last reviewed 25 July 2026; to be confirmed before the service opens publicly. The system is currently a pilot with no customers.

What we hold, and why

If you apply to join

Your organisation’s name and type, your name, email, and optionally phone and website, plus what you told us you would like. We use it to decide on and set up your account, and to contact you about it.

If you send us a message

Your message, and your name and email if you give them. Feedback sent from a map’s page also records which map it was about, so we can pass it on.

If you have an account

Your email, your name if given, your role, and which organisation you belong to. We sign you in with a one-time link rather than a password, so we never hold a password for you.

While you use the service

An essential sign-in cookie (no tracking, no advertising, no third-party analytics), short-lived server logs including your IP address to keep the forms from being abused, and a record of governance actions — who approved, published or updated a map, and when.

What we don’t do

  • No advertising, profiling or tracking cookies, and no analytics that identify you.
  • No selling or sharing of your details. Feedback about a map is passed to the organisation that publishes it, and nothing more.
  • No payment details: the service is currently free, and we take no card data.
  • No personal data on the public pages. A published map shows the publishing organisation, not any individual.

Keeping and removing it

We keep applications, messages and account records while your organisation uses the service, and the record of who approved or published a map for as long as that map is published — it is part of showing that public transport information was properly checked. Ask us at any time for a copy of what we hold about you, a correction, or deletion where we no longer need it: use the contact form. You can also complain to the UK Information Commissioner’s Office (ico.org.uk) if you’re unhappy with how we’ve handled your data.

Where the map data comes from

Bus services

Timetable and route data comes from the UK Bus Open Data Service (BODS), published under the Open Government Licence, and is cross-checked against operators’ own timetables.

Streets, rivers & landmarks

Geography comes from OpenStreetMap, © OpenStreetMap contributors, licensed under the Open Database Licence (ODbL). Every sheet we publish carries that credit.

Using the maps

Published sheets are meant to be used: print them, as many times as you like, at no extra cost — put them on a noticeboard, include them in a newsletter or a leaflet for your community. Two conditions come with that: don’t alter the sheet, and don’t remove or crop out the credits that appear on it — the geography and the bus data are both published under licences (OpenStreetMap ODbL, BODS OGL) that require attribution, so the acknowledgement has to stay wherever the map is used. And don’t present a map as live departure information — always point people to the operator or bustimes.org for times on the day.

Every map is reviewed by a person before publication — a check that it looks right, not an independent verification against timetables — and bus services change regardless; if you spot something wrong, tell us from that map’s page and we’ll look into it — though we’re a pilot run by one person, so we can’t promise how quickly.

The software

The portal itself is open source under the Apache 2.0 licence — github.com/PeterC66/community-bus-maps. No map data or customer information is held in that repository.
