Privacy, licensing & attribution

Short version: we hold only what we need to run the service, we set no tracking cookies, and the maps are built from open data that must be credited.

Last reviewed 25 August 2026. The service is currently a pilot with no customers.

Who is responsible for your data

BusMaps.uk is run by Peter Cooper, who is the data controller for everything described on this page. There is no other organisation involved and nobody else holds a copy. To ask anything about your data, or to exercise any of the rights below, use the contact form — it reaches him directly.

What we hold, and why

If you apply to join

Your organisation’s name and type, your name, email, and optionally phone and website, plus what you told us you would like. We use it to decide on and set up your account, and to contact you about it.

If you send us a message

Your message, and your name and email if you give them. Feedback sent from a map’s page also records which map it was about, so we can pass it on.

If you have an account

Your email, your name if given, your role, and which organisation you belong to. We sign you in with a one-time link rather than a password, so we never hold a password for you.

While you use the service

An essential sign-in cookie (no tracking, no advertising, no third-party analytics), short-lived server logs including your IP address to keep the forms from being abused, and a record of governance actions — who approved, published or updated a map, and when.

What we don’t do

  • No advertising, profiling or tracking cookies, and no analytics that identify you.
  • No selling or sharing of your details. Feedback about a map is passed to the organisation that publishes it, and nothing more.
  • No payment details: the service is currently free, and we take no card data.
  • No personal data on the public pages. A published map shows the publishing organisation, not any individual.

Keeping and removing it

Applications and messages are deleted after 24 months. That happens automatically, whether or not anyone remembers — the one exception is an application from an organisation that went on to become a customer, which we keep for as long as that account exists, because it is the record of how the account came about and of the checks we made before opening it. Once the account goes, the 24-month clock starts.

Account and publishing records last longer, on purpose. Who approved or published a map is kept for as long as that map is published: it is how we can show that public transport information was properly checked before people relied on it.

Ask us at any time for a copy of what we hold about you, a correction, or deletion: use the contact form. If you ask us to delete something we will do it, and we will also tell you the date the last backup holding it rotates out — backups are kept for up to 90 days and cannot be edited after the fact, so that date is when the deletion is genuinely complete rather than when we press the button. If you have an account, deleting your form submissions does not close it; say so and we will do both.

You can also complain to the UK Information Commissioner’s Office (ico.org.uk) if you’re unhappy with how we’ve handled your data.

Where the map data comes from

Bus services

Timetable and route data comes from the UK Bus Open Data Service (BODS), published under the Open Government Licence, and is cross-checked against operators’ own timetables.

Streets, rivers & landmarks

Geography comes from OpenStreetMap, © OpenStreetMap contributors, licensed under the Open Database Licence (ODbL). Every sheet we publish carries that credit.

Stop names & stand letters

Where a sheet shows which stand or bay to stand at, that letter comes from NaPTAN, the national stop register kept by the Department for Transport, published under the Open Government Licence — the same letter as the one on the physical flag.

Using the maps

Published sheets are meant to be used: print them, as many times as you like, at no extra cost — put them on a noticeboard, include them in a newsletter or a leaflet for your community. Two conditions come with that: don’t alter the sheet, and don’t remove or crop out the credits that appear on it — the geography and the bus data are both published under licences (OpenStreetMap ODbL, BODS OGL, and NaPTAN OGL where a boarding plan is shown) that require attribution, so the acknowledgement has to stay wherever the map is used. And don’t present a map as live departure information — always point people to the operator or bustimes.org for times on the day.

Every map is reviewed by a person before publication — a check that it looks right, not an independent verification against timetables — and bus services change regardless; if you spot something wrong, tell us from that map’s page and we’ll look into it — though we’re a pilot run by one person, so we can’t promise how quickly.
