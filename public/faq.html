Frequently asked questions

Can’t see your question? Ask us — we’re happy to help.

What does “pilot” mean here?

It means the system is built and working end to end, but it has never been used in anger. Specifically, and plainly:

  • We have no customers. No organisation has signed up. Every map on this site was produced by us, to build and test the system.
  • The example maps are ours. They are real output — nothing is mocked up — but no council or business publishes them, and they are not maintained as live travel information. Anything shown as Sample is one of these.
  • There is no service level. No uptime promise, no response times, no guarantee the monthly refresh happens on time. It is run by one person alongside other work.
  • Things may change or stop. We may alter how it works, pause it, or withdraw it. We would give reasonable notice and you would keep the sheets you had already downloaded.

What is not provisional: the maps themselves are built from official open data, cross-checked, and signed off by a person before publication. The pilot label is about the service around them, not about the care taken over them.

When we have organisations using it and a track record to point at, this notice and the banner at the top of every page come off.

What is BusMaps.uk?

A system that produces clear, printable bus maps for a place — a whole town or parish (an area map), or a single point such as a shop, school or centre (a place map) — and is designed to help you keep them current as services change. It is currently a pilot.

Who can apply?

Any organisation whose community uses the bus: councils, shops, businesses, schools, event organisers, charities, attractions and more. Every application is reviewed and approved before an account is set up. During the pilot we are looking for a small number of organisations willing to try it and tell us what is wrong with it.

How much does it cost?

Nothing. It is free during the pilot. We may introduce charges in future, but always with notice — you would never be billed by surprise.

How many maps can I have?

To start with, each organisation can have one area and a few places. Each map is approved individually. We would expect to raise these limits once the pilot has run its course.

What can I change myself?

You can choose which outputs you want, recolour and relabel routes, turn landmarks on or off, and accept the monthly updates — then download print-ready files. The more delicate layout work (straightening lines, nudging labels) we do for you, so the result reads properly in print.

What are the four outputs?

An internal geographic map (drawn to the streets), an internal schematic (straightened and tidied), an internal diagram (tube-map style), and an external map showing where the buses go. You pick which ones you want for each map.

How up to date are the maps?

The intention is a refresh from official data every month, and that is how the system is built to work — though during the pilot the cadence is not guaranteed. When something changes on your routes, you’ll see “a change to review”, preview it, and accept it — your colours and labels carry over automatically.

Where does the data come from?

Bus routes, stops and timetables come from the UK Bus Open Data Service (BODS), published under the Open Government Licence. Streets, rivers and landmarks come from OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL). Live times should always be checked with the operator or at bustimes.org.

Does our map get a public web page?

Yes. Once a version is signed off, it gets a page on our public site — the sheets to view or download, your organisation’s name, badge, colour and website, and a form so readers can tell you if something looks wrong. It also appears in our published maps gallery. You control your public details, and you can take a map off the public site with one tick without affecting your own copies.

Can I print them?

Yes — every map is produced as a print-ready A4 sheet at 300 dpi (and as an SVG), sized and coloured for real printing.

Do you guarantee they’re correct?

No. A person reviews and signs off every map before it can be printed, and we build only from official open data — but timetables change, and this is a pilot with no warranty behind it. These maps are a guide: please encourage readers to confirm live times before travelling.

What do you do with my details?

We use them only to consider your application and to set up and support your maps. We don’t sell them or use them for anything else, and you can ask us to delete them.
