Clear, printable bus maps for your community

A system designed to help local organisations publish easy-to-read maps of the buses in their area — or the buses that reach their doorstep — and keep them up to date as services change. It is a pilot: we are looking for our first organisations.

Who it’s for

Most often, it’s a town or parish council. But the same problem — the buses are there, and nobody can see them on one sheet — shows up for these too.

🏛️

Transport authorities & councils most common

One sheet showing every operator’s buses — the multi-operator information an Enhanced Partnership scheme can call for, kept current without agency fees. County, district, town and parish.

🏥

Healthcare, campuses & schools

Fewer missed appointments, less pressure on the car park. Patients, visitors, students and new staff all arrive not knowing the local network.

🎪

Town centres, BIDs, tourism & attractions

Get visitors from the station to your door without a car. Day-trippers will not download an operator app — they will read a printed sheet.

🚌

Bus operators & community transport

Turn your own open-data feed into a print-ready network map, without paying for vector artwork every time a route changes.

Not on this list? Business & science parks, shops, village halls, event organisers, surgeries — if your community relies on the bus, the same map helps. Ask us.

Each of these tends to have a different budget that this comes out of — during the pilot there is no charge, whichever one is yours. How a price would be worked out later.

Two kinds of map

Choose whichever fits — or have both.

Area

A whole place

A town, a rural parish, or part of a larger town — every bus within the area, drawn to scale with local landmarks.

Place

A single point

Centred on one spot — a shop, school, station, community centre or town centre — showing the buses that serve it and where they go.

Does it work on a big town?

St Ives is a market town and fits on a sheet easily. High Wycombe has three times the network — so it is the more useful proof.

Measured first, drawn second

Every area is scored before the drawing starts, on how many lines the map would carry, how tightly they bunch and how much of the network would end up hidden underneath other routes. High Wycombe came back RED — the honest verdict being “do not just build the standard sheet”.

Working through a documented sequence of remedies and re-scoring after each one brought it to GREEN, with nothing congested and no route buried. The map alongside is that build.

Five outputs — you choose which you want

Each map can produce any combination of these. Turn on only what you need.

🗺️

Internal — geographic

Street-anchored map of the buses within the area or around the place.

📐

Internal — schematic

A straightened, tidied version that’s easy to follow at a glance.

🚇

Internal — diagram hand-finished · extra

A tube-map-style diagram of the local network. The machine solves the topology; every line and interchange is then placed by hand — and re-placed whenever the network moves. Real drawing time, so it is asked for, not ticked.

↗️

External

Where the buses go — a diagram of routes to their destinations.

🚏

Where to board by arrangement

The four above answer where do the buses go. The fifth answers the question you have while standing in the bus station: I have decided to go to Bedford — which of these five identically-named stops do I wait at? It is a large-scale plan of one place, with an index of the destinations you can reach, listed alphabetically by destination rather than by route number, each row naming the stop to stand at.

Every stop letter is printed from NaPTAN, the national stop register, so the letter on the sheet is the letter on the flag. Where the stops around a place cannot each be named unambiguously, the sheet declines rather than guess — so it is checked for your place first. See one · what it is, and where it works

How it is meant to work

Apply & get approved

Tell us who you are and what you’d like. We approve your organisation and your first area or places.

We set it up

We build your map from official open data and give it a once-over before it comes to you.

You fine-tune

Adjust colours and labels, choose which outputs you want, and preview.

Approve & print

Every map is reviewed before it goes out. Download a print-ready A4 sheet — and accept updates as services change.

Built to be checked

✅

Human review

People rely on these to catch a bus, so a person looks over every map — and approves it — before it can be printed.

📶

Official open data

Built from the UK Bus Open Data Service and OpenStreetMap, with a monthly refresh cycle.

🎨

Readable by design

Route colours drawn from a recommended colour-blind-friendly palette, and generous type, sized for real printing.

Or take it on yourself

This is looking for a Community Interest Company

The system works, it is documented, and the code is safely held under a licence that converts to fully open-source in 2030. What it does not have is an organisation around it — it is one person’s project, and a map that lasts only as long as one person keeps maintaining it is not really maintained.

So we are looking for an individual or a small team to take it on and run it as a CIC: sustainable transport, GIS or civic tech, with the drive to lead it.

What would be handed over, why a CIC, and what is not yet resolved.

Why we are saying so publicly

Because the alternative is a service that quietly depends on one person and does not say so. Every organisation considering this deserves to know how it is run before it puts a map on a noticeboard.
