Complex towns

St Ives fits on one sheet without much argument. High Wycombe does not — and pretending otherwise is how you end up with a map nobody can read.

What makes a town hard

Three things, mostly: a lot of services, a dense centre where they all converge, and long through-routes that cross the whole map. The failure they cause is specific — lines end up drawn underneath other lines, so a route is on the sheet but invisible.

Rather than find that out at the end, the system measures it at the start: how many lines the map would draw, how many stops, how much of the area carries five or more routes at once, and how far that congestion stretches.

Scored before the work starts

Those four measurements produce one verdict — GREEN build it normally, AMBER apply some remedies first, RED stop and choose a strategy.

It runs on the survey, before the expensive drawing work, which is what lets a quote be based on the actual difficulty of your town instead of a guess. How that feeds into a price.

High Wycombe: RED → GREEN

Scored as it came out of the data, High Wycombe failed all four measures at once: 31 lines and 320 stops on one A4 sheet, over a square kilometre of the map carrying five or more routes, and congestion running right across it. Two-thirds of the typical route was buried under another one. That is a red verdict, and the honest response to it is not to build the sheet.

The fix is a documented sequence, applied in order and re-scored after each step: move the rarest services to a text list rather than drawing them; merge the route families that run along the same road into one line; stop routes at the edge of the town-centre box instead of tangling them through it; draw only the stops that are interchanges or termini; and where there are still more lines than there are distinguishable colours, colour by corridor and let the badges carry the route numbers.

The re-score came back 11 colour groups, 91 stops, nothing congested and nothing buried — inside the range of the towns that were straightforward from the start. Same town, same data, and no service left off the sheet — the rarest are named in the services list instead of being drawn as lines. The map above is the result.

The point is not that High Wycombe is difficult. It is that we can tell you it is difficult before anyone starts drawing, and that there is a written procedure for what to do about it.
